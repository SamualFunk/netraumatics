== Vegetation | Contemplation ==

As a sidebar, and one related to my exploration of nothing, alienation and immensity (see The Poignant Nothing), I figured I should present as a man of action. One who walks the walk and as you've read obnoxiously talks the talk. So in a manner of which I hope to expand on in that series, and maybe perhaps getting ahead of myself here, I want to share my approach to living, some basic thoughts and attitudes for the day to day. 

First and foremost, my ethos to life is to try to consider each action in terms of impact. It's not as exhausting as it may seem, since I prefer inaction and that is hardly effective at generating any sort of readable impact. I'm sure you've already assumed I'm lazy by that preceding statement, as inaction is the primary means of being so. But I like to consider this inaction as more vegetative than lazy. A occupation of seemingly doing nothing but passing time, and in that passing time engaging in contemplation; don't act, just think. In my experience, something beautiful emerges with reverie, a sort of centering that doesn't provide immediate objective truths, but eternal reflection. An action resulting in almost retroactive impacts, a way to consistently redefine my experiences and psyche by engaging with whatever image comes to mind and following where it leads. I'd say in capacity this writing is a reflection of these daydreams. A text which may or may not speak to you, my dear reader, but which I feel is relevant to share as a way of starting with nothing as zero-point. 

Recent events have made it a priority to me to do little but think about our placement in this world. As I've said previously, things are dreadful. We live in a world which cannot support humanities' willful trajectory (western capitalistic telos to be exact). This world spends every second of every day reminding you as such. The morbid symptoms we see...widening wealth gap, snowpack isn't what you recall as a child, the soft-fascist blabbering nonsensical statements about greatness etc...I'd say, as a moment of light advice giving, put things down. Take a moment to engage in reverie and by means of enforced boredom if that's what it takes. You're expected not to; you're expected to engage in all elements of productivity that reinforce this trajectory. You're expected to dream apropos progress and think that, for example, a life on Mars, brought to you by SpaceX, is not only possible (it isn't) but an inevitability of human development. Or to dream that machine intelligence will offer a Jetsons-esque existence of robot servants that will cure your cancer and do your dishes all while canceling your unneeded subscriptions is the fantasy to strive for. Instead, try contemplation, isolated and 'unproductive' contemplation. Daydreams emerge when you allow yourself the time and space to have them, but obviously the choice is yours.

One of my many inspirations, Plotinus, supposed nature embodied itself by being in continual contemplation. Nature is in contemplation of itself. We seem to occupy an inside/outside relationship with what we deem to be nature, being one moment a part of it, yet radically different than everything else it has produced; we're seemingly an exception. The materialist sciences purport it to be in causal relationship with itself, something which may be understood, observed and controlled through complex systems and interplays which mankind may be able to manipulate. While we may find such systems existing, in observation we know much less than we'd like and know even less so about any potential ability to control it. This is especially so since in environmental studies, we'd have to argue these finding through a wall of justification for profiteering and hope for continual growth.  While I'm no environmental scientist and only softly mystical with the whole thing, I know one thing: we have to become used to consuming much less than we do currently to preserve what we hold nature to be.      

Yes, yes I know individual lifestyle change is the least impactful...it's the corporations, the government, etc. We've all heard this cynical attitude. I'd at least like to offer the individual the dignity of having an impact, instead of throwing a person in the waste bin of indifference and consumerist hedonism of 'better enjoy it while it lasts'. Besides, we're the ones who keep these companies afloat often so by confusing wants and needs. Of all the worries I have and choose to contemplate, the changing climate is by far what concerns me the most. It's going to get worse, much worse. With it many people are going to die and the rest of us are going to be in situations of pure survival. The disavowal in our society is my primary concern regarding so. We know we have to consume less meat, we know we have to travel less, buy less throwaway junk...yet we feel either helpless or that the situation is futile, or in true fashion - 'I know but nonetheless I will continue to do so' qua enjoyment. And yes, I'm aware such changes in lifestyle are challenging, perhaps impossible especially for the less fortunate and the under-served in society. But if you're able to make such changes, and you occupy the substantial consumptive segment of society (professional-managerial class let's be honest) I implore you to do so. This isn't intended to be a moralizing statement, whether you eat meat or don't doesn't much concern me; the production of such is the problem. It is something that is massively intensive to grow to fruition and all whilst expecting it for breakfast, lunch and dinner. The same issue rests with our disavowal of AI, we know how awful it is for both the environment and our psyche, yet we continue to use it for trivial tasks and outsource our basic thinking for a word-salad that projects itself as thought. Or the consumption of fast fashion and junk and so on. 

To me, within this kernel of ecological disavowal, is a false belief that if we simply find another way to continue this path and cut carbon outputs that nature can emerge victorious once again. We must cut through this notion that nature is able to rebound, it may very well not.  The idea that things will self-correct after a period of turbulence so that we can rest easy is a component of our passivity and disavowal. We must at least come to terms that you or I may very well be victims of this, and not in some far off future. In contemplation of nature and ecology, we may reduce our inside/outside feeling to an imaginative inclusion, even if the nature emerging is not what we nostalgically recognized as such. We only really recognize our place in lost fantasy, which is why we so often seem occupied with a return to something that never truly existed. I recognize myself as prior in retrospect, but that projection is of the moment and in contemplation I can generate both past and future. Might as well be the latter.   

If you're able and willing, strive for less and engage with less, you'll find a certain 'much more' with this new found time to contemplate. I do not intend to push moral positions of my beliefs, which always suggest a right and a wrong. Right and wrong often rest in cylindrical form, breaching each others boundaries and blurring the lines of where one starts and ends. I simply wish to promote the purpose of willful contemplation as perhaps a means of living, a vegetative contemplation to counter the many issues of global capitalist alienation and how we find our placement in this world. It doesn't have to be the aforementioned topics. Engage in art, music, poetry, literature or simply nothing. We don't have answers, but fantasy. We can think first and act second with intention and preparation. We can engage our thoughts with imaginative insight, develop a story and play with even the most dreadful scenarios to discover the point in which we're obliged to act and grow. This can be in relation to yourself or to the world around you. In and through contemplation will follow an ethical point of departure; the zero point.
