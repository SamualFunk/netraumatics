 Consensus among the masses is this year has been a calamity (as were the years prior in the topsy-turvy 2020s). In gazing at our ever changing horizon, one can't help to wonder what sundowning we may face as a singular or collective of nations. Is it painful to view the coming atrocities humankind is facing as apocryphal? Perhaps a sudden break that leads to the collective extinction of humankind? This horizon is of course vast and blinding, yet unpredictable as to what may come. We have ideas of how we may die perhaps in no time at all: nuclear annihilation, rogue AI hacking machines, zoonotic diseases or maybe a combination of the likes. Regardless of its speed, we are in an age of rapid decline and particularly so in the United States. I find it unhelpful to view the coming tribulations as an extinction-level event in which we must all accept fate. History progresses ever so, albeit in manners which may obscure any indications of hope. To live through history is to tarry with this unknowing, and to seal this progression as trending towards an inevitable ending is well below the potential of our will and dignity. At the risk of sounding optimistic, and to use an absolutely vulgar phrase - now is the time to pull ourselves up by our bootstraps. 

Managing the apocalypse is indeed a vast initiative, yet I remain convinced our inevitable extinction is perhaps some time away. That's not to outright reject the idea that we're inhabiting an apocalyptic time, it is by all definitions just that. Of whatever rests within the immensity of these coming events is a singular given component - our inherent alienation as subjects. How are we expected to 'individually' rest within ourselves and furthermore conspire to manage our situation when a truly insightful deep-dive into the self produces a eerie uncertainty? This look within of course a task as immense as our current situation, but the production of nothing is preferable to the production of nothingness. Within the boundaries of nothing we can come to understand an infinite horizon that supersedes the one in which we find ourselves gazing into; i.e. the objectively bad state our world is in and the future as it presents itself as now. In avoiding nothingness, I support leaving the doomers to their perversions or showing them the way to the nearest doomsday cult (if doom is what you enjoy, you'll have plenty of good company!). The poignant and preferable nothing I've mentioned is where I've found at least some modicum of direction for the times. It is in exploration of empty space can we come to provide the subject room to become a subject of the times.

You now occupy the expanse of nothing where severed heads fly and cows appear as black as the space they occupy, by what means are we provided to make sense of any of this? You look to a partner, who suggests the heads have been severed as punishment for hubris, perhaps the gods saw insult at their former whole using fire, a powerful threat to those above. The cows appear black because we now have no fire to see their intricate patterns and colors in this night. Everything begins to make sense as to why the world appears this way as the symbolic order begins to develop, but you have no access to the actuality of this world. Interpretation is mediated through mythos through which you begin to ascribe belief and reason, you accept these as fact and move on with your day in this new world thankful that at least you're not a prisoner in some cave. This is the aforementioned nothing through which we navigate our being in this world and through which we are alienated from actuality. In exploring mythos we can come to understand why we must see this as the zero-level for our navigation in these times. Do you accept or radically reject what is provided? Perhaps both choices are worse? This is our uncertainty and in it we can find the means to reinterpret this world and how we engage with it simply because we must as subjects. 

In this exploration we must come to terms with this 'actuality' of the world. The developed tools of human materialist understanding (sciences, mathematics, psychology etc.) can illuminate a passage, but not the entire text of a situation. A reduction to numerical value can very well obfuscate the actual *value* of the phenomena. For example, 38.1 billion metric tons of CO2 was emitted into the atmosphere by fossil fuel production in 2025 alone, an absolutely horrifying numerical value, but it can be manipulated to meet a societal *value*. What does it mean for us as a species that occupies this planet? For some, it's merely a byproduct of human development, a sign of the industrial machine whirring itself ever more as we seek to maximize our economic gain. For others, its a product of modernity, but will be overcome in future years as we seek to change our means of production to something more eco-friendly. What do we encounter in this continuation of the as-is or of the hypothetical; the could-be? These are, of course, posited through the framework of continual *economic* progress and both of which rest their values blanketed under this numerical value for justification. Is the climate changing *strictly* due to the emissions of greenhouse gases or at its core connected to the way in which we adhere to a social authority (the imperative of economic growth)? My inclination is to say the latter green-centric example is the more obscene of the two as it carries the most insidious *value* that human economic progress has a 1:1 replacement and that we can continue this trajectory with adjustments. But really, both camps are worse. If an off-switch for these emissions existed, should we simply flip it and witness collapse overnight? This hypothetical is core of the apocalyptic positioning we find ourselves in today, of which the impossibility of all scenarios is immense. 

We are obliged to find a solution to these issues that plague us today, albeit through the utter impossibility and helplessness we feel when confronted with them. We have to reckon that this is new territory for us as a species which offers no palpable, universal solution. I'm in the camp of radical rejection in this darkness, harboring a certain 'no that's not it' when looking at history proper. Our erroneous timeline has led us to this moment to act ethically and its in this vast nothing in which we can construct a mythos of something. We can't expect these to overcome these tribulations and arise a new society that 'figured it out all out'. Nor is there anything in which we can return to. Our alienation is strictly unknowing, but through the unknowing we can find our commitment. I look within myself and see nothing but a malleable subject of the working of society. I can take that nothing and reject the nihilistic nothingness or I can take this nothing and through it see the all encompassing nothing in which all other people occupy. Things will get worse, and we must brace ourselves for not only the present, but the near future. We must engage in honest contemplation of our commitments and priorities not through individual doom & gloom but through mythos that will benefit the whole. 

I plan to continue to explore alienation and immensity in further writings...
